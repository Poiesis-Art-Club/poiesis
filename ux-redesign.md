# Poiesis UX redesign — implementation brief

## Intent

The public experience should help a first-time visitor answer three questions in a few seconds: **What is Poiesis? What can I do here? How do I take part?** The existing medieval and Renaissance visual language stays, but the interaction model becomes an editorial guide rather than a collection of disconnected destinations.

## Information architecture

| User need | Destination | Primary action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand the club | **Get to Know Us** (`/about`) | Read the introduction and meet the team. |
| Find a creative starting point | **Studios** (`/create`) | Explore practical prompts and ideas. |
| Share or read member work | **Echoes** (`/echoes`) | Open the member archive or sign in. |
| Attend, propose or volunteer | **Join Us** (`/join`) | Message Poiesis on Instagram. |

## Page structure

The home page will pair a concise statement of purpose with a four-step pathway: **Discover, Make, Share, Take part**. A compact “Meet the people behind Poiesis” preview makes the team visible without requiring visitors to search for it.

The **Get to Know Us** page will be reworked into three editorial blocks: *The Club*, *What We Make Space For* and *Meet the Team*. Team cards will use the supplied individual portraits and only their source-confirmed roles and interests. The copy will remain in English.

The **Join Us** page will clarify the three existing routes (attend, propose, contribute) and add a distinct volunteer route. Suggested volunteer directions are session support, visual and media support, hospitality and logistics, and documentation. Every public call to action routes to the club’s official Instagram, rather than collecting unverified data in a new form.

## Interaction rules

The main navigation uses clear action labels, a direct member-access link and a responsive mobile menu. Primary calls to action use a consistent hierarchy: one main action per block, secondary links for exploration, and no dead-end page. Team cards use light lift and portrait motion on hover, while respecting reduced-motion preferences.

## Delivery constraint

Changes remain in the local project workspace and local checkpoint until the owner explicitly authorises a GitHub push or public deployment.
